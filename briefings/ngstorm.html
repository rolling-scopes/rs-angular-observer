Built (from PRs and diaries):

  • Sprint 1: Header, Footer, Angular Material setup, ESLint/Prettier config, auth service scaffold, CI file (PR #19), registration form with commercetools integration — OAuth2 flow, address management, auto-login after registration (PRs #18, #25, #31), authRequiredGuard with UrlTree redirect (PR #34).

Level: Strong technically and reflective. Her diaries are the most architecturally mature of the team: she explains not just what she did but why she refactored (e.g., switching auth state from customer-based to token-based because the customer wasn't loaded at initialization, causing incorrect sign-in redirects on refresh — diary 2026-06-15). She demonstrates understanding of the design implications of her choices, not just their implementation. Sprint-2 diary documents ~25h on the profile page alone. AI tool usage is not mentioned in her diaries — all solutions appear hand-crafted, which aligns with the sprint-1 mentor observation about depth.

Risk: Light. The one thing to probe is whether the postal-code directive and ControlContainer approach was her own design or inspired by an external source — the diary doesn't mention AI here, but the pattern is non-obvious.

Probe at interview: "Walk me through the authentication state refactor — why was the customer-based signal causing issues on page refresh, and how did switching to a token-based signal fix it?" (she documented this precisely in the 2026-06-15 diary). Then: "In your custom postal-code directive, how does ControlContainer let you access the parent form without passing it as an @Input? What would break if you injected it with {optional: true}?"
