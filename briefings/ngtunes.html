cherkasovaa (lead) — 118 commits / 22 PRs (22 merged) / 10 diaries

Built (from PRs and diaries):

  • Sprint 0: project bootstrap — angular2026q2 organization, repo, branch strategy, commitlint, ESLint, Stylelint with clean-order, Prettier, Husky + lint-staged, PrimeNG integration, Kanban board setup, team role assignment (PR #57 area, diary 2026-05-09).
  • Sprint 1: About Us page with team cards and an animated banner (PR #58); favicon update; SCSS fix and stylelint config corrections (PRs #59, #61, #62); project preset fix (PR #57). 4 diary entries for sprint 1 including a canonical commitment to the "small reusable components" principle.
  • Sprint 2: Artist page end-to-end with Swagger exploration and custom endpoint request to backend teammate (PR #68, diary 2026-06-02); Albums + Artists pages with CatalogComponent (shared dump component with discriminated union for CatalogData, effect() for accumulation, isInitialLoading / isLoading signal split, PR #71, diary 2026-06-04); auth guard using hasToken computed signal tied to TokenService (diary 2026-06-03); Library page with listening history (date-chip carousel using horizontal scroll, scrollIntoView), playlist creation with reactive forms and drag-and-drop reordering from @angular/cdk (diary 2026-06-22, sprint-3 date label but sprint-2 scope); loading/error states (PR #98); "Play all" button (PR #97); misc fixes (PRs #72, #76, #78, #105, #109, #111).
  • Sprint 3 overlap: InjectionToken + useValue/useFactory (PR #103); directives, pipes and forms (PR #101); Library page continued (PR #100).

Level: The most prolific feature contributor and the clearest diary writer on the team. Her entries are in Russian, substantive, and document genuine thinking: she catches herself starting implementation before understanding the API response shape ("I started writing functionality before understanding what I receive"), identifies when effect() is less appropriate than computed() for accumulation but explains why she used it anyway, and documents the hasToken reactive chain from first attempt (checking isAuthenticated(), which caused a race condition) to final solution (wrapping the token in a signal inside TokenService). The auth guard diary entry is one of the most technically grounded pieces of writing in the team. No explicit AI-use acknowledgment in her diaries — her work appears self-driven.

Risk: The Library page diary (2026-06-22) describes rushed architecture: "I started without thinking about structure and architecture, so I'm not satisfied with the architecture." The same diary says the page "took about a week" but she "returned to it day by day." She clearly values quality but time pressure affected the design. Also: as team lead she did not generate a design herself — the team used AI-generated mockups ("design generated by AI" appears in ShvetsBy's sprint-1 diary). Verify she can reason about design trade-offs, not just execute against them.

Probe at interview: Ask her to walk through the hasToken computed signal and why it solved the Library guard's timing problem — she documented the whole journey from broken isAuthenticated() check to the token-signal solution. Then: "In your CatalogComponent, you use a discriminated union type CatalogData — can you write that type definition from memory, and explain what discriminated union means in TypeScript?" Her diary names the concept explicitly, so this tests whether she owns it or just typed the words.

Level: The team's system architect. His backend diary (sprint-0 entry) opened with an architecture diagram of both frontend and backend before writing a line of code — and he stuck to it. The sprint-2 frontend diary is technically the most detailed writing on the team: it documents every problem and solution at the level of root cause (e.g., the Supabase singleton/RLS collision — diagnosed through Postgres logs, root-caused to in-memory session state on the service-role client, resolved by splitting into two Supabase clients). He understands what he is doing at a deeper level than most course participants: he cites the Angular 21 isActive function API as the Signal-native replacement for deprecated router.isActive(), then goes and implements it. His English technical writing (sprint-1 diaries) is precise and structured. No AI-use mention beyond the team's shared AI-generated design.

Risk: With 131 main-branch commits plus a separate backend repo, there is a real risk his sprint-2 interview scope is undefined — he built so much that a generic "what did you build?" question will produce a ten-minute monologue. The mentor should pick a specific system (auth modals OR the Supabase dual-client fix) and ask him to explain it from first principles. Also: backend deployment on Railway Free Tier had a keep-alive abuse issue he caught and fixed — ask what the failure mode was and how he diagnosed it. Tests whether he monitors production or just ships.

Probe at interview: "Walk me through the Supabase dual-client fix — what was the symptom, how did you diagnose it, and why did the service-role client start behaving like a user client?" (Entry #15, very specific problem-solution). Then: "What is the difference between sessionStorage and localStorage, and why does your TokenService check both on read instead of just one?" Tests whether the token persistence logic was reasoned through or copy-pasted.

Level: A solid mid-tier contributor whose work is real and complete. His diary writing is in Russian, more casual and bullet-pointy than his teammates', but shows genuine reasoning: he chose HttpClient + signals over httpResource in sprint 2 because Angular 21 still marks it experimental (noting it became stable in v22), then reconsidered after a mentor call and migrated in sprint 3 — that's a decision made with evidence, not at random. He also caught and resolved the SCSS breakpoint discussion (correctly explaining why CSS custom properties cannot be used in SCSS @media blocks). His commit count (49) is lower than teammates, but all 11 of his PRs merged, and the Home + Search features are complete and functional.

Risk: His diaries are less detailed than solidados and cherkasovaa. The sprint-2 search page diary reads more like a stream-of-consciousness working log than a technical post-mortem — it shows he was thinking through the problem live but does not demonstrate the same level of architectural reflection. The httpResource migration happened in sprint 3, so the sprint-2 defence may find him explaining the toSignal pattern rather than httpResource. Verify which API he actually used in the version he demoed for sprint 2.

Probe at interview: Ask him to walk through how the Search page handles the case where a user changes the genre filter: "Does the page make a new API request, sort the existing results, or both — and how does the code decide?" (He documented this split explicitly in the diary: genre/popularity = API request; name/date = array sort.) Then: "You chose toSignal over httpResource for the home service in sprint 2. What is the difference between them, and what did you gain or lose with each?" Tests whether the framework choice was deliberate.

The project scope is ambitious and mostly delivered: Angular 21 signals throughout, a real NestJS backend with JWT auth + Supabase, a working music player, search with filters, playlists with drag-and-drop. No inactive member, no silent branches, no red flags.

Strengths:

  • solidados is one of the stronger technical students in the cohort: backend + frontend, deep problem-solving documented at root-cause level, clean code architecture decisions.
  • cherkasovaa is a credible team lead: she bootstrapped the project, manages the Kanban, contributes the most sprint-2 features, and her diary writing shows genuine Angular concept internalization.
  • All three are active writers with diary continuity across sprints — rare for a three-person team.
  • GitFlow discipline is consistent: feature branches, PR reviews, semantic commits, CI gating.

Watch:

  • Perfect scores (100/100 twice) with brief sprint-1 comments raise a calibration question: verify depth with one specific Feynman exercise per student.
  • cherkasovaa's Library page diary flags rushed architecture — she is self-aware about it, which is a good sign, but check whether she can describe what she would do differently.
  • ShvetsBy's commit count is noticeably lower than teammates (49 vs. 118 and 131). His features are complete but fewer. The diary writing is thinner. This is not a red flag — the work exists — but the mentor should gauge whether his conceptual grasp matches the team average.
  • AI-generated design mockups are the team's UI foundation. No student acknowledges significant AI code generation, but it is worth asking briefly — the project complexity makes it unlikely to be purely AI-assisted, but the interview should include a small live exercise.

Recommended interview angle: Per student, pick one specific system they built and ask them to explain it from first principles — avoid generic "what did you build in sprint 2?" questions. For cherkasovaa: the hasToken auth guard chain. For solidados: the Supabase dual-client architecture. For ShvetsBy: the Search page request vs. client-side sort decision. The diary trail makes these questions specific and fair: you are asking about things they demonstrably thought through.
