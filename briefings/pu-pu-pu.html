  • savryna is the strongest Angular practitioner: she diagnosed OnPush, understood Zone.js performance, and extended the NestJS backend on her own in sprint 3.
  • shoblinsky has the most thorough diary trail — 8 entries — and is genuinely self-critical, which is more useful for long-term growth than confidence without calibration.

Watch:

  • The workload split is uneven: tryproxy 230 commits / savryna 130 / shoblinsky 76. This is partly appropriate (backend is genuinely complex), but shoblinsky's lower output means the mentor should verify that he understands the features tryproxy built — not just the ones he shipped himself.
  • shoblinsky has open plans in his sprint-3 diary (TrackRow migration, search routing with query params, toaster) that were not completed by the deadline. Clarify what is actually merged vs. intended.
  • tryproxy's Angular-specific depth: he is clearly in his element on backend architecture. Make sure the Angular concepts he did implement (interceptor, provideAppInitializer, DI tokens) are understood at principle level, not just as functional code.
  • PR #114 (header refactor by savryna) and PR #115 (S3 storage by tryproxy) were open as of the snapshot date — confirm their status before the interview.

Recommended interview angle: For each student, take one concrete thing from their sprint-3 diary and ask them to explain it at the level of "why this approach, not an alternative." Savryna: NgZone.runOutsideAngular vs. (wheel) binding, or Zod DTO vs. TypeScript interface. tryproxy: TransactionRunner abstraction (options 1/2/3 trade-off), or provideAppInitializer failure mode. shoblinsky: signal forms vs. reactive forms trade-off, or the filterPredicate string-serialization constraint. Avoid abstract Angular theory — the diaries give you a precise set of things each person actually touched, and those are the right test surfaces.
