Source: repository Cryptangular/crypto-trade-ui (commits, PRs, dev diaries on the development-notes branch). Snapshot 2026-06-24. Goal: give the mentor context in 2 minutes before the defence — who did what, who understands it, what to probe.

One-liner: Green — all three members (aliakseiDrob, AnatoliRub, Zhdko) active through the sprint-2 deadline (last commits 2026-06-22/23), all scored 100 at both Sprint 1 and Sprint 2 defence rounds. The project is ambitious: Angular SPA with a real NestJS + PostgreSQL backend, Binance Spot Testnet WebSocket streams, and HttpOnly cookie auth. Notable stack choices: the team rolled their own UI kit (StxButton, StxToaster, StxInput, StxHeader) on top of Angular Material rather than adopting a third-party component library wholesale. The work is distributed — each person owns a coherent domain. There are no dropout risks and no significant knowledge gaps detected in the diary trail.

Strengths:

  • aliakseiDrob's full-stack auth work (JWT → HttpOnly cookie refactor, 401 disambiguation, refresh token endpoint) is the most technically sophisticated backend work on the team and is credibly documented.
  • Zhdko's diary writing is the most precise on the team: problem/solution format, named failure modes, code review responses, architectural self-correction. The TransitionEvent refactor is a standout example of understanding-driven debugging.
  • AnatoliRub's settings module — isolated domain zone, unified error format, HMAC validator — shows architectural thinking above beginner level, even if the first-pass implementation required multiple rewrites.
  • The team runs proper GitFlow (named feature branches, PR-per-feature, code review with mentor participation) and all four diary slots are filled by all three members.

Watch:

  • Coordination lag: aliakseiDrob's sprint-2 diary explicitly names the problem — "proposals to tighten sync went no further than likes in the channel." This has not caused a member to go silent, but several features are still open heading into sprint 3 (Markets page WebSocket, settings form, order book widget). If the team does not tighten sync, the final product may be a collection of partially-integrated modules rather than a working trading platform.
  • PR #32 (settings page): still open at snapshot. Anatoliy's most complex deliverable is in review. Ask whether it's close to merge or blocked.
  • Binance WebSocket workarounds: both aliakseiDrob (Markets page) and AnatoliRub (order book gateway) hit Binance API friction and both used public API as a fallback. Probe whether they understand the testnet limitations they encountered vs. their own implementation choices.

Recommended interview angle: All three students score 100 and have substantive diary trails — generic questions will not differentiate surface-level prep from genuine understanding. Use their documented failure-and-fix moments as the entry point: aliakseiDrob's 401 disambiguation, Zhdko's transitionend + Vitest fix, AnatoliRub's NestJS exception filter design. Each of these can be turned from "you documented this" into "explain it without looking at the code." That is the Feynman threshold test.
